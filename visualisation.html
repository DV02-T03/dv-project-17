Which jurisdiction recorded the highest number of speeding fines per 10,000 licences over the last five years (2020–2024), and how do the states and territories compare?

Over the five-year period from 2020 to 2024, Queensland (QLD) recorded the highest number of speeding fines per 10,000 licence holders, with a rate close to 48 fines per 10,000 licences.

Among the remaining jurisdictions, Western Australia (WA), the Northern Territory (NT), Tasmania (TAS), and the Australian Capital Territory (ACT) also show comparatively higher rates, all falling within roughly the 24–28 fines per 10,000 licences range.

In contrast, New South Wales (NSW), Victoria (VIC), and South Australia (SA) recorded substantially lower rates, with SA showing the lowest enforcement intensity of all jurisdictions.

Overall, the bar chart highlights significant variation across Australia:

  • QLD clearly stands out with the highest rate
  • Several jurisdictions sit in a mid-range band
  • NSW, VIC, and especially SA show noticeably lower levels of speeding fines per 10,000 licence holders

How has the total number of speeding fines and the proportion issued by cameras versus police changed from 2010 to 2024?

From 2010 to 2024, the total number of speeding fines shows clear year-to-year variation, but a consistent long-term pattern is visible. In the early 2010s, police-issued fines made up a larger share of overall enforcement, while camera-detected fines formed a smaller proportion.

Over time, this relationship gradually reversed. Camera-issued fines increased—both in absolute numbers and as a proportion of all speeding fines—while police-issued fines generally declined or remained comparatively stable.

Across the full period, cameras became the dominant method of enforcement, accounting for a growing majority of total fines in more recent years. As a result, the composition of speeding enforcement shifted from a police-led model to one increasingly driven by automated detection systems, even as the overall volume of fines fluctuated.

Did the patterns of speeding enforcement (total fines and fines per 10,000 licences) show a significant change during the COVID-19 period (2020–2021) compared to the years immediately before and after?

The chart shows a clear change in speeding enforcement during the COVID-19 period (2020–2021). Both total speeding fines and fines per 10,000 licence holders drop noticeably compared with the years immediately before the pandemic. This decline aligns with reduced travel, movement restrictions, and lower traffic volumes during lockdown periods.

After 2021, both measures begin to rise again, indicating a return towards pre-COVID enforcement levels as mobility recovered. Overall, the visual evidence suggests that COVID-19 had a substantial impact on speeding enforcement activity, with a significant temporary reduction during the pandemic period followed by a gradual rebound.

How do the trends in speeding fines per 10,000 licences for the two most populous states, New South Wales and Victoria, compare over the last decade (2014–2024)?

This visualisation compares the trends in speeding fines per 10,000 licence holders between New South Wales (NSW) and Victoria (VIC) over the last decade (2014–2024).

By expressing fines relative to licence holders, the chart highlights differences in enforcement intensity between the two states, showing whether fines increased, decreased, or remained stable across the period.

Overall, both states show clear year-to-year fluctuations, but NSW consistently records higher fines per 10,000 licence holders than Victoria across most of the decade. NSW's rate shows more pronounced rises and falls, while Victoria's trend is generally lower and more stable. This suggests that, relative to the number of licence holders, speeding enforcement has been stronger or more variable in NSW than in Victoria over the period.
